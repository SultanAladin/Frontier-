PROJECT DRIVE / TYRE DEFORMATION / 14 OCT 2026

The physics wheel stays rigid. The drawn tyre does not.

XPBD is gone. The trace-fan contact solver publishes one 128-byte record per wheel (hub, axle, patch centre, normal, deflection, Fx/Fy lean, tread speed, pressure) and the vertex stage bends whatever tyre mesh is on the car — slick or treaded — in world space, so the flat spot stands still on the road while the rubber rolls through it.

What this is: the actual ControlVehicle and the actual DriveSceneAuthor wheels, posed by the actual VehicleSolver, bent by the CPU port of the very shader text the GPU runs (Engine/Shaders/TyreDeformation.slang). Flat diagnostic palette, no GI. What it is not: a Vulkan screenshot — no GPU is available here; the three shaders that include it compile to SPIR-V (34/34 green).

The rigid shell sinks into the road by exactly the solver's deflection (the physics wheel is a rigid disc of nominal radius); the bent tread never crosses the ground plane, and every frame asserts both. Deflections are honest: 12 mm on a 330 mm tyre is what a road car does, so look at the flat spot, the belly beside it and the difference panel rather than expecting a cartoon.

Inspect the actual frames

All clips start paused. Play one to pause the others. Left: what the raster drew before. Middle: what the vertex stage draws now. Right: absolute pixel difference ×3 — black means the two agree, so it isolates the patch, the sidewall belly and the fore/aft or sideways lead.

Static load: four flat patches

Dropped from 2 cm onto flat ground and left to settle. ~3.0 kN per front tyre, 12 mm deflection, ~11 cm patch half-length: a flat spot and a slight sidewall belly, nothing moving. Per-wheel trace.

Hard brake from 25 m/s

Full brake at 0.4 s. Load shifts forward (fronts to ~4.1 kN, 16 mm), the tread leads rearwards under −4.7 kN of Fx, and everything relaxes back as the car stops at ~2.5 s. Per-wheel trace.

Left corner at 18 m/s

Steer ramps to 55 % left. The outside front carries ~4.4 kN with 18 mm deflection and 4.8 kN of Fy; the carcass walks ~30 mm sideways while the patch stays on the road. Per-wheel trace.

Speed humps at 8 m/s

Four 35 mm sine² humps from x = 4 m. The trace fan tilts the contact normal with the road, so the flat spot leans into each hump face and the deflection pulses as the tyre swallows it; the ground mesh is the same height function the solver traced. Per-wheel trace.

Half, nominal and double inflation

Three identical cars, cold gauge 55 / 110 / 220 kPa. Radial stiffness now follows the air (k = k0·(0.25 + 0.75·p/p₀), TraceContactTyre.h), so the soft tyre squashes ~1.7× deeper and bellies more, the hard one sits taller with a shorter patch. No rigid panel: every panel is deformed. Per-wheel trace.

How it is wired

  • ProjectTyreDeformationInterchange (versioned C export, Project-Drive and Project-Zero) → TyreDeformationSequence in FrontierRuntime resolves wheel placements to instance rows once and stamps InstanceFlagTyreDeform | slot << 16.
  • VisibilityExchange keeps 64 [current, previous] record pairs in one SSBO (raster set binding 4, resolve binding 16) refreshed with vkCmdUpdateBuffer only when the revision changes.
  • VisibilityRaster.vert bends this frame's and last frame's positions (motion vectors stay right), ShadowRaster.vert bends the shadow caster, SurfaceResolve rebuilds the same bent triangle so normals and wire agree.
  • Rim, hub and brake are untouched: the weight is 0 inside RimRadius and smooth-steps to 1 at the tread, so any authored wheel works as long as its rim is rigid.
  • Pressure now reaches the physics as well as the picture: PressureStiffness() scales the trace-contact radial stiffness with the live gauge pressure (thermal state when it runs), so a hot or over-inflated tyre rides taller and a soft one squashes deeper before the shader ever sees the record.
  • Not bent (yet): ray-query / BLAS geometry and the rest SDF — a ≤ 2 cm discrepancy in reflections and GI contact shadows, listed in the plan.
