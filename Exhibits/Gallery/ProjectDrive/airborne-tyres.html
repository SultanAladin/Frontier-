Your stable service-brake experience is consistent with the baseline measurements. The previous source did not generally have broken service brakes: all measured moving service-brake stops passed. 2. Airborne tyre: concentricity, inflation and attachment are different

Bead seating alone did not answer your observation. This new check measures the centre of the actual outer tread relative to the rigid rim, the radius growth caused by inflation/spin, and the circumferential variation within each tread ring. It uses the production XPBD tyre, closed native tyre projection, actual pressure-surface beads and authored rigid rim. The actual vehicle freefall/tumble cases include gravity and suspension, with no ground, drive or chassis damping.

No sustained, visibly hanging tread was reproduced on the current source.

The actual vehicle's settled centre offset is about 0.05–0.07 mm; the maximum recorded all-node centre offset during tumbling is about 0.121 mm. Averaging all nodes can hide variation across the width: the worst individual-ring centre offset is 0.879 mm, and late circumferential variation reaches 2.934 mm at this fast tumble rate. These are reported rather than forcing a perfect circle. By contrast, mean inflation changes the outer radius by about 4.2 mm. A rigid rim must not deform along with the pneumatic tread. Different-looking inner and outer contours do not by themselves demonstrate detachment.

The controlled pre-compressed wheel begins with 38.553 mm of loaded node deformation and 149 contact nodes. After release, contacts are zero, the maximum offset drops to 8.200 mm after one 4.167 ms step and about 6.250 mm after two steps. The remaining few millimetres are primarily inflated shape, not a carcass hanging away from its rim. No shape clamp or artificial rigidification was used.

3. Why apparently stable landings can still flag energy

The original phrase meant that the brake/rim check did not certify the separate landing energy budget. It did not mean that these brake images visibly demonstrate an instability. The current source has now been rechecked in all eight historical spinning-drop conditions: initial centre-of-mass height 9 m, upward velocity 8 m/s and initial angular speed 8 rad/s, flat ground, disconnected drive and no applied brake. 4/8 pass the complete necessary-screen set.

The diagnostic sums body/particle/rim kinetic energy, referenced gravity, gas/carcass/suspension elasticity and ground-compliance potential. The biggest peaks are dominated by the ground-compliance potential calculated from transient particle penetration; the tyres remain within the shape bounds and the motion can still settle afterwards. A short impact-time excess can be invisible in a 5 Hz clip. This is an unresolved impact/contact-coupling or energy-accounting issue, not evidence that the service brakes fail. The account remains unclosed, so this report does not claim exact whole-vehicle conservation or identify a final cause.

One diagonal landing also records a small positive static-contact-power peak above the existing 1 W screen. That result is preserved in the audit rather than removed to make the report green.
